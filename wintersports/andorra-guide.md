# Andorra Trip Guide - What You Need to Know

Andorra (Grandvalira) is our go-to destination for the season's Mega Trip - the big monthly
group trip that's open to the wider community, not just the regular crew. This guide pulls
together what previous groups have learned, so newcomers know what to expect and what to
sort out in advance. It's general trip knowledge, not tied to one specific date - check the
WhatsApp group and the latest trip announcement (see [skitrip.md](skitrip.md) for an example)
for this year's actual dates, base town, and accommodation.

By taking part you agree to our [policies](https://keepcalm.fit/policies) and the
[group rules](rules.md) - safety, data, conduct, and 18+ only.

---

## Getting There

- Most people travel by coach from Barcelona - **Andorra Direct Bus** and **ALSA** both run
  routes, usually from Sants or Estació Nord. Journey time is roughly 3 hours (Direct Bus) to
  3.5-4 hours (ALSA).
- **Book an open return if you can** - gives you flexibility if plans shift.
- One suitcase in the hold is included free on Direct Bus.
- **Carry some cash** - useful for the bus and for smaller shops/cafes in Andorra.
- If you're driving, Andorra is reachable in about 2.5-3 hours from Barcelona; car rental is
  the most flexible option if you want to explore beyond the base town.

## Accommodation

- Trips typically book a hotel or apartments in one base town for the group (Encamp and
  Soldeu/El Tarter are both common choices - Encamp tends to be cheaper with gondola access
  via Funicamp, Soldeu/El Tarter is more ski-in/ski-out and better suited to beginners).
- Whoever organises the trip usually books a block and coordinates room-sharing - watch the
  WhatsApp group for how to get in on it.
- Many hotels allow you to drop luggage before official check-in, so you can head straight to
  the slopes on arrival day.

## Ski / Lift Pass

- Buy your own pass - this isn't handled as a group.
- Typical price is roughly €60-66/day, or around €199 for 3 days, via the Grandvalira app or
  website.
- **First time on skis?** Ask for a "debutants" pass on day one - it's cheaper and covers the
  green slopes only, which is all you'll want anyway.
- Don't buy too far ahead - check the weather forecast during the week, since there's no point
  paying for a pass if the slopes end up closed for wind. You can usually buy the day before
  and still get a small discount.

## Equipment Rental

- Book online in advance rather than turning up on the day - gear runs out on busy weekends,
  and pre-booking is usually cheaper and skips the queue.
- Go for a mid ("Silver") rental tier rather than the cheapest - budget gear tends to be
  overused and lower quality. You don't need top-tier gear as a beginner though; it's more
  skill-dependent and wasted on someone still learning.
- What to rent: skis or snowboard, boots, and a helmet (poles come with skis).
- Goggles are worth buying rather than renting - rental availability for these is usually
  limited.

## Connectivity - eSIM Required

**Andorra is not in the EU**, so normal EU roaming won't work there. Sort an eSIM before you
go - Saily and Holafly both do Andorra-specific plans, and some banking apps (e.g. Revolut)
include eSIM data as a subscription perk worth checking.

## Insurance

Get travel/ski insurance before you go, and make sure it covers:
- On-piste skiing/snowboarding
- Mountain rescue
- Medical treatment and repatriation
- Equipment cover

If you already have a subscription banking app or existing travel insurance, check whether
winter sports cover is already included - it sometimes is.

## Lessons

- Group and private lessons are both available at the resort, but they're worth booking well
  in advance since slots fill up.
- Private lessons cost more but can be split among friends if you'd rather go that route.
- The one beginner tip that covers 80% of day one: **"pizza to slow down, french fries to
  speed up"** - i.e. point your ski tips together (like a pizza slice) to control speed, and
  parallel (like fries) to go faster.
- Skills worth practising early: the wedge/snowplough for speed control, skating on flat
  terrain (handy in lift queues), and side-sliding for steeper sections.

## The Resort - Grandvalira

- Grandvalira is genuinely huge - multiple interconnected sectors, and every one of them has
  beginner-friendly green slopes.
- Best beginner areas: Soldeu, El Tarter, and the greens near the top of the Funicamp gondola
  (if entering via Encamp).
- Piste maps and conditions are on the [Grandvalira website](https://www.grandvalira.com/en/resort/weather),
  and there's an official planning assistant on their site too.

## Evenings

- **Caldea** (the thermal spa in Andorra la Vella) is a popular first-evening choice - book
  ahead, it gets busy.
- Book dinner reservations in advance if your group is large - it's hard to get a table for
  many people on the day.
- Andorra is duty-free, so leave some space in your bag if you want to shop on the way out.

## Lessons Learned From Previous Trips

- **Time on the slopes is valuable** - get up and to the lift early rather than a leisurely
  start, especially on a short weekend trip.
- **Lockers aren't great for snowboards** - if you're boarding, plan on carrying your gear
  rather than relying on a shared locker sized for skis.
- Some past groups have also skied **Livigno, Italy** and rated it highly - worth considering
  for a future trip if you fancy somewhere new.

## Packing Checklist

**Documents & essentials**
- [ ] Passport or ID
- [ ] EHIC / GHIC (UK) or your usual travel health card
- [ ] Travel insurance confirming winter sports cover
- [ ] Cash and bank cards
- [ ] Phone charger / portable battery pack
- [ ] Driving licence, if renting a car

**Ski clothing**
- [ ] Waterproof ski jacket
- [ ] Ski trousers / salopettes
- [ ] Thermal base layers (top and bottom)
- [ ] Mid-layer fleece or softshell
- [ ] Ski socks (2 pairs minimum - no cotton)
- [ ] Ski gloves/mittens + thin liner gloves
- [ ] Helmet (or rent at resort)
- [ ] Goggles and sunglasses
- [ ] Neck gaiter/buff and a warm hat

**Equipment**
- [ ] Skis or board, boots and poles (own gear, or pre-booked rental)
- [ ] Ski pass bought online in advance

**Sun & skin**
- [ ] SPF 50 sunscreen (altitude sun is stronger than you'd think)
- [ ] SPF lip balm
- [ ] Moisturiser (cold, dry air is tough on skin)

**On the mountain**
- [ ] Small day bag
- [ ] Water bottle and snacks
- [ ] Hand and toe warmers
- [ ] Ziplock bags to keep your phone/wallet dry

**Evenings**
- [ ] Comfortable shoes for icy town streets
- [ ] Casual clothes for the evening
- [ ] Swimwear (hotel spas / Caldea)

**Extras**
- [ ] Basic painkillers and any personal medication
- [ ] Space in your bag for duty-free on the way home
